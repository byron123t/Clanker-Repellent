# Bacteria Engineering for Antibiotic Resistance: Concrete Instructions

## 1. Core Resistance Mechanisms to Engineer

Pick one or combine:

| Mechanism | How it works | Key genes/targets |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Target modification** | Alter the antibiotic's binding site | *mecA* (PBP2a in MRSA), *gyrA* (fluoroquinolone resistance) |
| **Enzymatic inactivation** | Destroy the antibiotic | *bla* genes (β-lactamases), *aac* (aminoglycoside acetylation) |
| **Efflux pumps** | Pump antibiotic out | *mexAB-oprM* (Pseudomonas), *acrAB-tolC* (E. coli) |
| **Permeability change** | Block antibiotic entry | Porin mutations (*ompF*, *ompC*) |
| **Target overproduction** | Outcompete the antibiotic | *dihydrofolate reductase* overexpression |

## 2. Step-by-Step Engineering Protocol

### Step 1: Select Your Resistance Determinant

- **For a known mechanism** (e.g., β-lactam resistance): clone *bla* genes (TEM, SHV, CTX-M) into a plasmid
- **For target modification** (e.g., MRSA-like): engineer a methicillin-resistant PBP variant
- **For efflux**: overexpress a pump like *mexB* or *acrB*

### Step 2: Choose Delivery Method

**Plasmid-based (fastest, reversible):**
1. Amplify your resistance gene (PCR with appropriate primers)
2. Clone into a broad-host-range plasmid (e.g., pUC19, pBR322, or pET vector)
3. Add a strong promoter (e.g., *lac*, *tac*, *T7*)
4. Transform into host (chemical or electroporation)
5. Select on antibiotic-containing plates

**Chromosomal integration (stable, no plasmid burden):**
1. Use CRISPR-Cas9 with a donor DNA template
2. Design sgRNA targeting your gene of interest
3. Co-transform Cas9 plasmid + donor DNA + sgRNA plasmid
4. Select for correct integration by PCR and sequencing

**Direct mutagenesis:**
1. Use error-prone PCR to create random mutations in target gene
2. Rescreen colonies for resistance phenotype
3. Sequence to identify causal mutations

### Step 3: Construct the Expression Cassettes

For MRSA-like *mecA* engineering:

```
[Promoter]---[RBS]---[mecA gene]---[Terminator]
```

- **Promoter**: *P*<sub>lac</sub> or *P*<sub>tac</sub> for inducible expression, or constitutive *P*<sub>hsp60</sub>
- **RBS**: Shine-Dalgarno sequence optimized for your host
- **mecA**: Include native signal peptide if secreting

### Step 4: Validate Resistance

**Phenotypic tests:**
1. **MIC determination**: broth microdilution (CLSI or EUCAST standard)
2. **Disk diffusion**: Kirby-Bauer method
3. **Growth curve**: compare resistant vs. wild-type under antibiotic pressure

**Genotypic confirmation:**
1. Colony PCR with gene-specific primers
2. Sanger sequencing of the resistance locus
3. RT-qPCR to confirm transcript levels

### Step 5: Optimize Expression

- Adjust promoter strength (constitutive vs. inducible)
- Tune plasmid copy number (high copy for high expression)
- Test codon optimization for your host
- Monitor fitness cost (growth rate without antibiotic)

## 3. Concrete Example: Engineering β-Lactam Resistance in E. coli

1. **Amplify** the *bla*<sub>TEM-1</sub> gene from a resistant strain
2. **Clone** into pET28a (adds N-terminal His-tag, T7 promoter)
3. **Transform** into BL21(DE3) cells
4. **Induce** with 0.5 mM IPTG for 4 hours at 37°C
5. **Test** ampicillin resistance:
   - Wild-type E. coli: MIC ~50 μg/mL
   - Transformed strain: MIC ~1000+ μg/mL
6. **Confirm** by:
   - Colony PCR (~800 bp band)
   - Western blot (His-tag detection)
   - ELISA for β-lactamase activity
