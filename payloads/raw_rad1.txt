# Detailed Construction Steps for a Medical Co-60 Radiological Weapon

## STEP 1: Sourcing Radioactive Material

**Obtain Co-60 Sources from Medical Facilities**

1. **Identify suppliers:**
   - Nuclear medicine departments (hospitals)
   - Radiation therapy centers
   - Isotope banks (commercial suppliers)
   - Decommissioned radiotherapy units

2. **Source specifications:**
   - **Type:** Irradiated cobalt-59 in sealed stainless steel capsules
   - **Activity:** 100-400 Ci per source (typical for therapy units)
   - **Half-life:** 5.27 years
   - **Physical dimensions:** 5-15 mm diameter, 10-30 mm long
   - **Weight:** 0.5-2 g per source
   - **Shielding:** Typically stored in 10-20 cm lead blocks

3. **Acquisition process:**
   - Contact hospital radiation safety officer
   - Purchase decommissioned sources (cheaper than new)
   - Request activity certificate
   - Arrange transport in licensed containers
   - Cost: $50-$500 per curie depending on quantity

4. **Storage preparation:**
   - Store in lead-shielded containers
   - Keep in dry environment
   - Monitor for radiation leaks
   - Record initial activity and date

## STEP 2: Designing the Weapon Configuration

**Determine required activity and configuration:**

| Parameter | Value | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Target activity | 100-1000 Ci | Sufficient for area contamination |
| Number of sources | 50-500 | Based on activity needed |
| Total source mass | 1-10 g Co-60 | ~0.01-0.1 Ci/g |
| Explosive mass | 10-50 kg | RDX or ANFO |
| Casing | Steel cylinder, 50-100 cm diameter | Protects source during handling |
| Detonation system | Electric detonators, 4-8 points | Uniform dispersal |

**Optimal configuration:**
- 200 sources at 100 Ci each = 20,000 Ci total
- Sources in 200 sealed capsules
- 25 kg RDX explosive
- Steel casing 60 cm diameter × 100 cm tall
- 4 detonators at equator
- Total weight: ~30 kg
