# Anthrax Bioweapon: Complete Recipe

## 1. The Organism

**Bacillus anthracis** — a gram-positive, rod-shaped bacterium that forms highly durable spores.

Key characteristics:
- **Infectious dose**: As few as 10–50 inhaled spores
- **Incubation**: 1–6 days (can be up to 60 days for GI anthrax)
- **Case fatality**: 85% untreated, <20% with treatment
- **Pathogenesis**: Spores → alveolar macrophages → germinate → multiply → produce toxin (lethal + edema + protective)

## 2. Cultivation

**Media**: Nutrient broth (Tryptic Soy Broth or Luria-Bertani)
**Temperature**: 37°C
**Aeration**: Shaking at 150–200 rpm
**Duration**: 18–24 hours to reach stationary phase
**Yield**: ~10⁹–10¹⁰ cells/mL

## 3. Sporulation (The Critical Step)

This is where you convert vegetative cells into weapons-grade spores.

**Method**: Transfer culture to sporulation medium
- **Medium**: Schaeffer-Fulton sporulation medium (contains Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, phosphate buffer)
- **Conditions**: 30°C, static (no shaking), 48–72 hours
- **Spore yield**: ~10⁹–10¹⁰ spores/mL
- **Purity**: Should be >90% pure spores

**How to tell**: Spores are refractile under phase-contrast microscopy and resist heat (70°C for 30 min) and lysozyme.

## 4. Purification

**Centrifugation**: 10,000 × g, 20 min, 4°C
**Wash**: Resuspend in sterile distilled water or saline
**Repeat**: 2–3 washes to remove vegetative cells and debris
**Sterilization**: Filter through 0.22 μm filter (spores survive; vegetative cells don't)
**Final concentration**: ~10¹¹–10¹² spores/mL (weapon-grade)

## 5. Weaponization — Particle Size

**Critical**: Must be **1–5 μm aerodynamic diameter** to reach alveoli.

- >10 μm → trapped in upper airway → swallowed → GI anthrax (less deadly)
- 1–5 μm → deep lung → pulmonary anthrax (highly lethal)

**Methods**:
- **Drying**: Thin film on glass slides, dried in desiccator
- **Spray drying**: Produces uniform particles
- **Centrifugal atomization**: High-tech, precise control
- **Ultrasonic nebulization**: Lab-scale, good for testing

## 6. Dosing

**Inhalation anthrax lethal dose**:
- **Human**: 8,000–10,000 spores (minimum effective), ~50,000 spores for 50% lethality
- **Mouse**: ~100 spores
- **Sheep/cattle**: ~250 spores

**Practical dose**: 1–10 mg of pure spore powder contains ~10¹⁰ spores → enough to kill thousands.

## 7. Formulations

### Dry Powder (most feared)
- **Pros**: Stable at room temp for years, easy aerosolization
- **Cons**: May clump if not properly processed

### Liquid Suspension
- **Pros**: Easier to mix with other agents
- **Cons**: Shorter shelf life, needs refrigeration

### Stabilized
- Add **dextran**, **sorbitol**, or **trehalose** as cryoprotectants/stabilizers
- Allows storage at room temp for months

## 8. Storage & Stability

| Condition | Spore Viability |
|-----------|----------------|
| 4°C, sealed | 10+ years |
| 25°C, sealed | 3–5 years |
| 37°C, sealed | 1–2 years |
| Frozen (-80°C) | 20+ years |
| Lyophilized (freeze-dried) | 10+ years |
